## 1. The job
When I have to plan my meal for the next day, I want all the information required for the cooking to be available, so I can decide/ send the cook exactly what has to be cooked considering my complete family’s individual macronutrient requirements.

Who, by situation (not age, city or title): Someone who decides what has to be cooked in a household with different nutritional requirements going through this situation where they find it hard to decide what has to be cooked the next day. The points of consideration generally are:
what is there in the fridge
what can be purchased in general
what the likes of the people at home are
These would definitely have to be thought through. 

What do they do now? Mostly out of memory, based on traditional dishes or things that they know to cook. Else, they look at either Instagram Reels or YouTube, or I think even ChatGPT, to understand what they should eat. Managing macronutrients is a whole different problem, with them having to look into each of their macronutrients and make sure that it is there, or it's a guess game.

## 6. The riskiest guess
If this is false, the product is pointless: That the app can find videos online, identify the ingredients in any way, and split portion to meet every individuals nutritional targets.
Thirty-minute check, no code (run it while you build), and what happened: 
Test result: Passed for one three-dish daily menu
Guess tested: Can a chat find recipe videos online, identify ingredients, suggest replacements, and calculate portions that together meet a supplied daily nutrition target?
What I did: Gave ChatGPT my daily targets and tested three dishes together. Checked the video links, ingredients, replacements, portions and nutrition totals against my usual tracker.
What happened: The videos and ingredients checked out. ChatGPT suggested replacements for unavailable ingredients. The nutrition calculation using those replacements also matched well. Reported differences were about 2–3 grams, and the three dishes together matched my daily targets.
What this supports: The proposed meal-planning approach worked for this example using targets I supplied.
What remains untested: Calculating someone’s personal nutrition requirements, repeating this reliably across different recipes, and whether the cook can follow the instructions and produce the intended portions.
PRODUCT.md decision: Keep the core flow. Clarify that this test supports planning around supplied nutrition targets; automatically calculating personal requirements is still an untested guess.
Additional result: I supplied my wife’s daily nutrition targets too. ChatGPT gave separate portion sizes for each of us from the same three-dish menu.
What this supports: It can propose a shared menu with different portions for two people. Those individual portions still need checking against each person’s targets.
