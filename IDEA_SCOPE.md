IDEA LOCK · Build Sprint

The idea, in one line:
An AI meal planner for households with a cook that turns available groceries and each person’s macro targets into tomorrow’s menu, a grocery list, and a shareable cook page.

Why me:
I feel this problem myself. I can’t cook, I have a cook, and every evening my wife and I have to decide what tomorrow’s meals should be using what’s in the fridge.
I have been into working out and fitness for a while and my extended family and my colleagues have a reputation of me as someone who is into fitness and a little bit of nutrition to a very large extent. They come to me with their queries so they do trust me. Tried to create a workflow with ChatGPT and it took way more than 10 attempts to create the impact itself. It did not really give me a breakup of macronutrients within the household and it made mistakes with the amount of groceries that were remaining. 


GOAL

The one goal they hire it for (life):
Consistently meet our household’s macro targets with less mental effort deciding what to tell the cook.

Delta 4 — today:
Check the fridge → browse saved Instagram recipes → mentally assemble meals around protein → send recipes to the cook → order missing groceries when the cook arrives → check macros after cooking.

Delta 4 — with my product:
1. Get an evening reminder, check the fridge, and update what’s available. Household targets and preferences are already saved.
2. Review tomorrow’s suggested menu, estimated macros for each person’s portions, cooking instructions, video links, and missing groceries.
3. Confirm the menu and order the missing groceries.
4. Share the read-only meal page with the cook on WhatsApp.

The improvement:
Make meal and grocery decisions the night before, with portions planned against each person’s macro targets before cooking.

The sin it rides:
Sloth — reduce the daily effort of planning meals and explaining them to the cook.

USER

The trigger:
The evening before the cook comes, when we need to decide tomorrow’s meals and what groceries to order.

Today’s path:
My wife and I check the refrigerator, browse saved Instagram reels, and plan one solid protein source per meal. We mentally assemble the rest around our macro goals. We often forget to order groceries until the cook arrives. I track the food on Healthify after it is made.

PRODUCT

Onboarding:
Enter the number of people and each person’s macro targets, allergies, and veg/non-veg preferences. Enter what’s in the fridge and receive the first suggested menu, estimated macros, and grocery list.

The core loop:
Update available groceries → review tomorrow’s menu and portions → confirm the menu and order missing groceries → share the cook page.

The cook page:
A read-only web page with tomorrow’s menu, ingredients, simple cooking steps, and a “watch how to make this” link for each dish. I share it with the cook on WhatsApp.

Coming back:
An evening reminder brings me back to plan the next day. Household targets and preferences stay saved.

The AI-first part:
AI creates a menu and portions using available groceries, household preferences, allergies, and each person’s macro targets. Macro values are estimates and depend on the cook following the specified portions and ingredient quantities.

Scope:
Include evening reminders and saved household preferences.

Exclude workout/form coaching, nutrition plans from body vitals, medical history, consumption tracking, wearables, HealthifyMe sync, grocery capture from photos, and a paid tier.

Defer weekly grocery predictions and perishability-based ordering until there is enough usage data to support them.

MARKET


Competitors:
ChatGPT and saved Instagram recipes are alternatives in my current workflow.

I tested ChatGPT with three prompts. In that test, it did not carry my fridge and household into the next day. Persistent household context is my proposed edge; whether that is enough differentiation remains unverified.

Distribution hypothesis:
A “made with” line on every cook page could introduce the product to other households because cooks often work in several homes. This is unproven.
